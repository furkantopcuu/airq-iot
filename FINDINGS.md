# Findings

What the experiments found, in plain English, including what did **not** work. Every number below is
read from a file under [`reports/`](reports/) (table names are given in brackets); the tables are
generated by `python -m scripts.make_tables`, never typed by hand. The reasoning, protocols and the
full decision log (in Turkish) are in [docs/KARARLAR.md](docs/KARARLAR.md).

**Setting.** Beijing Multi-Site Air-Quality data, 12 stations, hourly, 2013-03-01 to 2017-02-28
(35,064 hours per station; 2.1 % of PM2.5 missing; mean 79.8, 95th percentile 242 µg/m³)
[`dataset_summary`]. Chronological split: train 2013-03 to 2015-08, validation 2015-09 to 2016-02,
test 2016-03 to 2017-02. All fitting uses train, all choices use validation, and the test year was
evaluated once per protocol. The system is a trace-driven emulation: real measurements replayed by
virtual devices, not a field deployment.

## RQ1 - how well can PM2.5 be forecast?

Test-year RMSE in µg/m³ (improvement over persistence in brackets) [`rq1_main`]:

| Model | 1 h | 6 h | 24 h |
|---|---|---|---|
| Persistence | 19.65 | 54.88 | 88.12 |
| Seasonal naive | 88.14 (-349 %) | 88.16 (-61 %) | 88.12 (0 %) |
| Ridge, own station | 18.54 (5.6 %) | 50.05 (8.8 %) | 73.66 (16.4 %) |
| LightGBM, own station | 17.85 (9.1 %) | 48.88 (10.9 %) | 73.53 (16.6 %) |
| LightGBM + 3 neighbours | 16.99 (13.5 %) | 47.89 (12.7 %) | 73.35 (16.8 %) |
| LightGBM + 11 neighbours | 17.08 (13.1 %) | 46.60 (15.1 %) | 73.29 (16.8 %) |

- **Significance.** Diebold-Mariano tests per station with Holm correction: ridge and every LightGBM
  variant are significantly better than persistence at all 12 stations at each horizon; seasonal
  naive is not better anywhere [`rq1_stats`].
- **What the neighbours add** [`rq1_ablation`]: against the single-station LightGBM, 11 neighbours
  lower RMSE by 4.3 % at 1 h (significant at 10 of 12 stations) and 4.7 % at 6 h (12 of 12), but only
  0.3 % at 24 h (0 of 12). Nearby stations carry short-range information (what is arriving), not
  next-day information.
- **Held-out stations** [`rq1_loso`]: when a station is left out of training, the mean improvement
  over persistence is 12.6 / 14.5 / 17.0 % (1 / 6 / 24 h), against 13.1 / 15.1 / 17.0 % when it is
  included; the model improves on persistence at all 12 stations at every horizon.
- **What the model uses** [`rq1_shap_top`]: the current PM2.5 value dominates (mean |SHAP| 43.1 at
  1 h, 15.3 at 6 h, 8.8 at 24 h); the neighbour mean becomes the second feature and grows relatively
  at 6 h (13.8), with the neighbour minimum next (11.2).

**Negative result: extreme events** [`rq1_extreme`]. For targets above the training 95th percentile
(232 µg/m³) the models do not beat persistence at short range and fall behind it at long range. RMSE
for persistence vs LightGBM (11 neighbours): 40.5 vs 41.4 at 1 h, 117.2 vs 124.4 at 6 h, 171.1 vs
216.3 at 24 h. The mean error (true minus forecast) is positive, i.e. the model *underestimates*:
12.0 (1 h), 91.0 (6 h) and 193.0 µg/m³ (24 h) for LightGBM. A squared-error objective regresses
towards the mean, and the worst episodes are the ones that matter most for warnings.

## RQ2 - early warning

A warning is raised when the forecast exceeds `theta - delta`; the safety margin `delta` is chosen on
validation by maximising F2 (a missed exceedance costs more than a false alarm). The test year has
724 exceedance events above 150 µg/m³ and 43 regional haze episodes (at least 8 stations above 150 µg/m³
for at least 6 h) [`episodes_test`, `rq2_warning`, `rq2_haze`].

At theta = 150, LightGBM with 11 neighbours [`rq2_warning`]:

| h | Precision | Recall | F2 | Events caught | Persistence F2 / events caught |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 h | 0.865 | 0.955 | 0.936 | 97.9 % | 0.930 / 100 % |
| 6 h | 0.568 | 0.876 | 0.790 | 96.4 % | 0.762 / 100 % |
| 24 h | 0.482 | 0.528 | 0.518 | 72.9 % | 0.545 / 100 % |

- At 1 h and 6 h the learned model is a little better than persistence on F2 with fewer false alarms
  (0.070 vs 0.094 at 1 h; 0.181 vs 0.218 at 6 h).
- **Negative result at 24 h:** persistence has a *higher* F2 (0.545 vs 0.518) and catches every event,
  while LightGBM catches 72.9 %. This is consistent with the underestimation of peaks seen in RQ1 (we
  did not isolate the cause). The regional haze episodes show the same pattern: LightGBM warns for 39
  of 43 at 24 h, persistence for 43 [`rq2_haze`].
- Median lead time for the events LightGBM caught is 5 h (1 h horizon), 15 h (6 h) and 19 h (24 h)
  [`rq2_warning`]. The horizon-matched columns in the same table are the stricter measure: matched
  event recall is 0.66 / 0.80 / 0.53 at 1 / 6 / 24 h.

## RQ4 - how uncertain is the forecast?

Quantile LightGBM models (0.1 / 0.5 / 0.9) give a [q10, q90] interval with a nominal 80 % coverage.
A single widening factor `c` is fitted on validation only. Test-year results [`rq4_coverage`]:

| h | Coverage raw | Coverage calibrated (c) | Coverage on extreme targets | Mean width (µg/m³) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 h | 0.761 | 0.798 (1.10) | 0.774 | 27.0 |
| 6 h | 0.756 | 0.797 (1.10) | 0.621 | 85.0 |
| 24 h | 0.758 | 0.809 (1.05) | 0.321 | 143.6 |

- Overall coverage is calibrated to the target, but it is **not conditional**: on extreme targets it
  falls to 0.62 at 6 h and 0.32 at 24 h. A single scalar cannot fix an underestimating model.
- **q90-based warning vs the point-forecast warning** (theta = 150) [`rq4_warning`]:
  at 1 h there is no gain (F2 0.937 vs 0.936, false alarms 0.112 vs 0.070); at 6 h F2 is equal (0.791
  vs 0.790) with higher recall (0.940 vs 0.876) and more false alarms (0.261 vs 0.181); at 24 h recall
  rises from 0.528 to 0.862 and F2 from 0.518 to 0.623, with false alarms 0.404 vs 0.173. The q90
  warning is a recall-for-precision trade, useful mainly at 24 h.
- The protocol and the hypotheses were committed before the test year was evaluated (commit `0d7d495`).

## RQ3b - faulty sensor inputs and the edge filter

Faults (spikes, stuck values, dropouts) were injected into test inputs; the numbers are the RMSE
increase on rows whose previous 24 h contain a fault, with the edge filter off and on
[`rq3b_robustness`]:

| Model | h | Clean RMSE | Damage, filter off | Damage, filter on | Recovered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Persistence | 1 h | 20.42 | 45.74 | 7.27 | 84.1 % |
| LightGBM (own station) | 1 h | 19.04 | 27.00 | 18.96 | 29.8 % |
| LightGBM + 11 neighbours | 1 h | 18.09 | 22.36 | 14.65 | 34.5 % |
| LightGBM + 11 neighbours | 6 h | 48.40 | 2.34 | 1.54 | 34.1 % |

- Faults hurt most at 1 h and little at 24 h (damage 0.57 µg/m³ for LightGBM + neighbours).
- Neighbour features make LightGBM less sensitive to a faulty own sensor (damage 22.4 vs 27.0 at 1 h).
- **The filter helps a naive model a lot and a learned model much less.** We did not test why; one
  hypothesis is that the filter replaces a flagged value by "missing", which removes information the
  model would otherwise use.

## Senaryo Kontrol - the live system under scripted interventions

Seven scenarios were run once each, with the expected outcome written down before running
[`scenarios_summary`]: **10 of 11 checks were confirmed, 1 was refuted** (a stuck sensor with the edge
filter off was expected to raise the 6 h error above 12.57 µg/m³; it measured 10.54).

Paired runs apply the *same* fault window with the filter off and on, over three start dates
[`scenarios_ab`] (3 windows per fault type; no significance tests):

| Fault | MAE 1 h, off | MAE 1 h, on | MAE 6 h, off | MAE 6 h, on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spike, 3 h (mean of 3) | 94.4 | 49.1 | 24.5 | 40.6 |
| Stuck, 12 h (mean of 3) | 39.7 | 44.4 | 51.1 | 51.4 |

- The filter helps 1 h forecasts under spikes in two of three windows (mean gain 45.2 µg/m³) but
  **hurts 6 h forecasts** (mean -16.1). We did not isolate why; one reading is that nulling a value
  removes information the 6 h model would otherwise use. Under a stuck sensor the filter is slightly harmful at 1 h
  (-4.7) and neutral at 6 h (-0.3).
- An instant 300 µg/m³ jump is flagged by the edge `rate_jump` rule and nulled. That is correct
  behaviour for a sensor fault but surprising for someone expecting a pollution event; the dashboard
  offers `ramp` for a realistic rise.

## RQ3a - the system under message loss, delay and offline devices

57 runs (loss 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 % x delay 0 / 200 ms x two replay speeds x 3 repetitions, plus 1 / 3 / 6
offline devices) on one machine [`rq3a_bench`, `rq3a_bench_offline_fix`].

- **Delivery** matches the configured loss (0.946 / 0.899 / 0.803 delivered at 5 / 10 / 20 % loss);
  device-to-database latency is a median 2 ms without delay and about 208 ms with 200 ms emulated
  delay; no duplicates.
- **Forecast damage** (paired RMSE increase at 1 h, mean over 3 repetitions): +0.55 (5 %), +1.68 (10 %)
  and +5.60 µg/m³ (20 % loss); the 6 h and 24 h changes are within noise. 200 ms of delay changes no
  forecast (shorter than the 500 ms the predictor waits for a missing station).
- **Real-time headroom.** At 3600x (one simulated hour per second) the predictor keeps up with no
  loss and mostly with 5-10 % loss (median warning latency 1.1-1.5 s) but fell behind in 4 of 6 runs at 20 %
  loss (about 116 s latency) and in 1 of 6 runs at 5 % (6 runs per loss level: two delay settings, three
  repetitions each). The cause is the 500 ms wait for a missing
  station, which uses up the one-second budget per hour on hours with loss.
- **A flaw the benchmark found.** The same wait made a permanently offline device add 500 ms to
  *every* hour: with one device offline the median warning latency at 36,000x was 148.9 s against 38.5 s
  for the full network. The predictor now stops waiting for a station whose retained status says
  `offline`, after a short grace period (the status message can overtake the station's last readings
  through the edge). Re-measured on the final code: 37.0 s with one device offline (39.9 s for the
  full network), 30.2 s with three, 18.2 s with six; no run fell behind. Forecast quality is
  unchanged. The fix does **not** help with message loss: a lost message announces nothing, so that
  wait remains.

## What these results do not show

- **Reference-grade data, not low-cost sensors.** The stations are regulatory instruments; low-cost
  sensors drift and respond to humidity, so fault behaviour in the field will differ.
- **One city, one test year.** No claim about other cities or other years.
- **Application-layer network emulation.** A lost message is never published; there is no OS-level
  `tc netem`, so MQTT retransmission and TCP queueing effects are not measured.
- **One machine.** Absolute timings are machine-specific; the relative effects (loss, offline devices)
  are what the benchmark is for.
- **Small samples in the live scenarios.** One run per scenario and three windows per paired fault;
  treat them as demonstrations, not statistics.
- **Replay of a recorded year.** At each hour the system uses only information available at that hour
  (the leakage rules enforce this), but it has not been run on a real, unfolding data stream.
- **No authentication.** The command channel is for localhost; see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
